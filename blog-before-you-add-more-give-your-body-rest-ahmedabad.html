Before You Add More, Give Your Body a Rest

By NOSH7 Kitchen & Nutrition Team · 6 min read

We live in a world where we are constantly told to add something to our diet. Add a herbal tea, add a multivitamin, add a protein powder, add a superfood, add protein rich atta, add a detox drink, or add another supplement. The list never seems to end. But before you add something new, there is a simple question you should ask yourself: Have I given my body enough rest first?

We have too many choices today

A few thousand years ago, people lived with far fewer food choices. They ate what was available around them. They ate seasonal fruits, vegetables, grains, pulses, nuts and other local foods. Food was simple and mostly based on what nature and the local environment could provide.

Today, everything is different. We have hundreds of choices for almost every product, and almost anything can reach our home in 10 minutes. Want protein powder? Open your phone. Want herbal tea? Open your phone. Want vitamins, supplements, health drinks or a new superfood? It is probably already being advertised to you.

But just because something is available does not mean you need it.

Just because something is in the market does not mean your body needs it. And just because it can reach your home in 10 minutes does not mean it should become part of your daily routine.

Marketing has become very fast, but our body’s basic needs have not changed that much.

Your body needs simple food too

We often make healthy eating much more complicated than it needs to be. Good health does not always require a cupboard full of powders, supplements and special products.

For most people, a healthy diet starts with simple food. Fresh fruits, vegetables, whole grains, pulses, nuts and seeds, and good sources of protein can form the foundation of a good diet. The exact balance will be different for every person, but the basic idea is simple: eat real food, eat the right amount and eat a variety of foods.

We don’t need to turn every meal into a science experiment.

The hardest thing is not adding. It is removing.

One of the most difficult and most useful habits for improving your health is learning what to remove.

Adding something healthy is easy. You can buy a multivitamin, protein powder, herbal tea or a new superfood. But removing a bad habit is much harder.

Removing late night eating, unnecessary snacking, too much sugar, highly processed food or oversized portions requires something that no product can provide: a change in behaviour.

You may also need to remove the habit of eating simply because you are bored, stressed or because food is easily available. You may need to remove the habit of constantly searching for the next health product that promises to make you healthier.

These changes are not exciting. They don’t come in fancy packaging, and nobody delivers them to your home in 10 minutes.

But they can be far more important than adding another product to your diet.

Adding something healthy does not cancel a bad habit

This is where we often fool ourselves.

Drinking a herbal tea does not cancel overeating. Taking a multivitamin does not cancel a poor diet. Eating protein rich atta does not make an otherwise unhealthy diet healthy. Having a superfood does not cancel poor sleep.

Adding something good does not give us permission to continue everything that is bad.

Health does not work like this:

Bad habits + healthy products = healthy lifestyle.

It doesn’t.

Sometimes the first step towards better health is not adding anything. It is removing something.

Give your body some space

Your body is already doing a lot of work every day. It is digesting food, using nutrients, managing energy and constantly maintaining itself.

We don’t need to keep feeding ourselves simply because food is available. Giving your body reasonable gaps between meals, avoiding unnecessary snacking and getting enough sleep can help create a healthier routine.

This is not about starving yourself or following extreme fasting. It is about understanding that eating is only one part of health. Rest is another.

Your body needs good food, but it also needs sleep, movement and recovery.

What about supplements?

Supplements are not always bad. Some people genuinely need them because of a nutritional deficiency, medical condition, pregnancy, dietary restriction or another specific requirement. In those situations, they can be useful when taken appropriately.

The point is not to say that every supplement is useless.

The point is much simpler:

Don’t add something just because someone is selling it to you.

First look at what you are already doing.

Next time, ask a different question

The next time you see a new health product on your phone, don’t immediately ask, “Should I buy this?”

Ask yourself, “Do I really need this?”

Then ask, “Have I fixed my basic habits first?”

  • Have I been eating too much?
  • Am I eating when I am not hungry?
  • Am I eating too many processed foods?
  • Am I sleeping enough?
  • Am I giving myself enough time between meals?
  • Have I removed the habits that I already know are not good for me?

If the answer is no, maybe you don’t need to add another product.

Maybe you need to remove something first.

Final thought

Because sometimes the best thing you can give your body is not another powder, tablet, tea or superfood.

It is a little less unnecessary eating, a little less noise, a little more rest and a return to simple food.

The next time you feel like adding something new to your diet, pause for a moment.

Before you feed your body more, give it a chance to rest.
