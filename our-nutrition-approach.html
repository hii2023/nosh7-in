NOSH7 is not a diet fad. It is food designed for how people in Ahmedabad actually live and work today. Here is exactly what goes into a bowl, the principles behind it, and an honest account of what good food can and cannot do.

Why We Started With Nutrition, Not Salad

Traditional Indian food was built for a physically hard day: farming, walking, manual work. Most of us now sit for eight to twelve hours. The food stayed rich in refined carbs and oil, while the daily movement that used to balance it disappeared. The result across the city is the same quiet gap: too little protein, too little fibre, and too many empty calories.

So NOSH7 does not really sell salad. We sell a fix for that gap, one meal a day, made convenient enough to actually stick to. Every decision below follows from that single idea.

The Five Principles Behind Every Bowl

01

Protein comes first

Most people here fall short on protein, so we build the bowl around it: paneer, tofu, sprouts, legumes and seeds, from 18g in a standard bowl to 40g+ in the high-protein one.

02

Fibre in every bowl

The part most "diet food" forgets. Fresh vegetables, greens, legumes and seeds keep you full for longer and support steady digestion and energy.

03

Calorie-aware, not calorie-obsessed

A standard bowl lands around 350 to 450 kcal. Enough to satisfy, portioned so you are not hungry an hour later. We count so you do not have to.

04

Whole food over supplements

Real ingredients instead of powders and pills. Whole-food protein arrives bundled with the fibre, vitamins and minerals a scoop cannot give you.

05

Fresh is a nutrition decision

Nutrients and crunch fade with time and heat. We prepare each batch the same morning and deliver about an hour later, so the bowl reaches you at its best.

What Actually Goes Into a Bowl

  • A base of fresh greens and seasonal vegetables - the fibre and micronutrient foundation
  • A real protein element - paneer, tofu, sprouts, chickpeas, rajma or edamame (18 to 24g standard, 40g+ in the high-protein bowl)
  • A wholegrain or millet where the recipe calls for it, never refined flour as filler
  • A rotating mix of seeds - pumpkin, sunflower, flax, chia and sesame - for healthy fats, minerals and extra fibre, varied each meal
  • A light in-house dressing - for taste and balance, not drowned in mayonnaise or hidden sugar

What We Deliberately Do Not Do

  • No deep frying and no heavy gravies - the two things that quietly turn "healthy" food unhealthy
  • No hidden sugar or refined-flour bulking to make a bowl feel larger
  • No "detox", "fat-burning" or miracle-cure claims - none of that is real, so we do not sell it
  • No single one-size bowl - there are weight-loss, high-protein, low-sugar and Jain options so the food fits your goal, not the other way around

Being Honest About Health Claims

Balanced, portion-controlled, high-fibre meals can genuinely support goals like weight management, steady energy and better everyday nutrition. But food is food, not medicine. NOSH7 meals are not a treatment or cure for any medical condition. If you manage diabetes, thyroid, PCOS or anything else, our low-sugar and goal-based bowls are designed to sit alongside your doctor's or dietitian's guidance, never to replace it. Anyone with a specific medical condition, allergy or pregnancy should check with their own healthcare professional before changing their diet.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is NOSH7's nutrition approach based on?
Our bowls are built on widely accepted nutrition principles: adequate protein, plenty of fibre, whole-food ingredients and sensible portions, prepared fresh. We design for everyday nutrition and steady energy rather than fad diets or extreme restriction.
How much protein and how many calories are in a bowl?
A standard NOSH7 bowl is roughly 350 to 450 kcal with 18 to 24g of plant protein. The High-Protein bowl delivers 40g+ and the Weight-Loss bowl 30g+, so you can match the bowl to your goal.
Can NOSH7 meals help with weight loss or a health condition?
Balanced, portion-controlled, high-fibre meals can support goals like weight management and steady energy. They are food, not a treatment for any medical condition. If you manage diabetes, thyroid, PCOS or anything else, use our goal-based options alongside your doctor's guidance, not instead of it.
Do I still need protein powder or supplements?
For most people, no. Whole-food protein from paneer, tofu, sprouts, legumes and seeds comes bundled with fibre, vitamins and minerals that a powder does not provide. A high-protein bowl delivers 40g+ as part of a real meal.
